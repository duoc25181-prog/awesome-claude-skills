# Outbound Prospecting

## What this skill does

Turns a vague "find me customers" request into a small, checkable batch of real businesses that match an ICP, each with a short, specific outreach draft.

A batch is 8–15 businesses. Never more. Never a bulk list.

## Inputs you must collect first

Before searching, confirm all four. If any is missing, ask — do not guess.

1. **Industry / business type** — one specific type (e.g. "independent coffee shops", not "food businesses").
2. **City / area** — one city or metro area.
3. **One checkable trait** — something you can verify on the public web, e.g. "has no website", "website has no online ordering", "no Google Business Profile", "Instagram inactive for 6+ months", "menu is a PDF only".
4. **The offer** — one concrete thing the user sells (e.g. "a website with online ordering", not "marketing help").

## Hard boundaries

- **Draft only. Never send.** Do not contact a business by phone, DM, email, in person, or any other channel.
- **Public information only.** No private data, no bought lists, no scraped personal contact details.
- **Small batches only.** 8–15 businesses per batch, no bulk outreach.
- **Evidence or nothing.** Every signal in a draft must have a public source a reader can open and re-check. Unverifiable facts are dropped, not invented.
- Any later sending is a separate, human review-and-approve step outside this workflow.

## Workflow

### 1. Source pre-flight (before the batch)

- Identify 2–3 public source types for this industry + city (e.g. Google Maps listings, local directories, review sites, the businesses' own sites/social profiles).
- State which sources you will check and what each can prove.
- If a source type cannot be checked publicly, drop it and say so.

### 2. Store-by-store digging (never one big search)

- Work through candidate businesses one at a time, not in a single sweep.
- For each candidate, check: does it match the industry, is it in the area, and does it show the checkable trait?
- Keep only matches. Discard near-misses and say how many were discarded and why, in one line.
- Stop at 15 matches or when the batch is complete, whichever comes first.

### 3. Dual-source corroboration

- Every hard fact in the final output (business exists, location, trait, contact route) must be corroborated by two independent public sources, or flagged as single-source.
- A fact with only one source is either verified a second way or removed.
- Never present an unverified guess as a fact.

### 4. Drafting (one draft per business)

Each draft follows this structure, in this order:

1. **Signal-led opening** — one specific, verifiable observation about this business (with its public source).
2. **One mapped fix** — how the offer fixes exactly that observation, and nothing else.
3. **Free sample first** — offer a concrete free sample of the work, before any payment.
4. **Low-pressure close** — one easy reply, no urgency, no manipulation.

Constraints on every draft:

- Short: under 120 words.
- Specific to this business; a draft that could be sent to any business in the batch is a failed draft — rewrite it.
- No invented claims about the business, the user, or results.
- No pressure, no fake scarcity, no guilt.

### 5. Output format

Deliver a table with one row per business:

| Business | Why it matches | Evidence (public source) | Draft |
|---|---|---|---|

Then, in one short paragraph: what was checked, how many candidates were discarded, and what could not be verified.

## Anti-patterns (do not do these)

- Do not produce lists of 50+ businesses.
- Do not reuse the same generic draft across businesses.
- Do not infer a trait ("probably has no website") — check it.
- Do not add contact details you did not find publicly.
- Do not send, schedule, or queue any message.
- Do not continue to a second batch without the user asking for one.
